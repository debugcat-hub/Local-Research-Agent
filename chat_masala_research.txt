Chat Masala (also spelled "Chaat Masala") is a tangy, savory spice blend originating from the Indian subcontinent. It is a staple in South Asian cuisine, particularly in Indian, Pakistani, and Bangladeshi cooking. The name "chaat" comes from the Hindi word meaning "to lick" or "to taste," reflecting its mouth-watering quality.

Key Characteristics:
- Tangy and slightly spicy flavor profile
- Strong sulfurous aroma from black salt (kala namak)
- Used as a finishing spice, not a cooking spice
- Adds a "zing" to fruits, snacks, and street food
- Available as a fine powder, typically in shaker containers

Whole Spices & Seeds:
• Cumin seeds (Jeera)
• Coriander seeds (Dhania)
• Black peppercorns (Kali Mirch)
• Carom seeds (Ajwain)
• Dried mango powder (Amchur)
• Black salt (Kala Namak) — THE DEFINING INGREDIENT
• Regular table salt
• Asafoetida (Hing)
• Dried ginger (Saunth)
• Mint leaves (Pudina) — in some blends
• Dried pomegranate seeds (Anardana) — in some blends

Note: Every brand has its own secret ratio, but black salt and dried mango powder are always the dominant flavors.

3. USAGE GUIDE
━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━━

How to Use:
- Sprinkle on top of dishes RIGHT BEFORE serving
- Add to fresh-cut fruits (watermelon, mango, papaya, guava)
- Dust on chaat items (samosa, pakora, dahi vada, bhel puri)
- Use as a rim seasoning for cocktails (margaritas, bloody marys)
- Add to yogurt, raita, or chaas (buttermilk)
- Sprinkle on boiled eggs, popcorn, or roasted nuts

Dosage:
- For fruits: 1/4 to 1/2 teaspoon per serving
- For chaat: 1/2 to 1 teaspoon per plate
- For beverages: A pinch per glass

IMPORTANT: Always add at the END of cooking or just before serving. Heat diminishes the tangy flavor.

Best Dishes for Chat Masala:
