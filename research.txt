# 1. PRODUCT DESCRIPTION

## What is Chat Masala?
Chat masala (also spelled chaat masala) is a tangy, salty, and mildly spicy Indian seasoning blend that originated from the Indian subcontinent. It is a finely ground, ready-to-use spice mix that delivers a complex flavor profile combining sour, salty, spicy, and umami notes.

## Origin and History
- The name comes from "chaat" - a family of savory Pakistani and Indian street snacks
- It has been a staple in Indian cuisine for centuries
- Particularly associated with Delhi-style street food traditions
- Used primarily as a finishing spice rather than a cooking spice

## Key Characteristics
- Appearance: Brownish-red hue
- Texture: Finely ground powder
- Flavor Profile: Tangy, salty, savory, mildly spicy
- Usage: Sprinkled on top of food before serving (finishing spice)
- Shelf Life: Up to 1 year when stored properly

# 2. INGREDIENTS

## Common Ingredients in Chat Masala

### Whole Spices (typically roasted before grinding):
1. Cumin Seeds (Jeera) - Provides deep, earthy base flavor
2. Coriander Seeds (Dhania) - Adds citrusy brightness and complexity
3. Fennel Seeds (Saunf) - Introduces mild sweetness and cooling note
4. Black Peppercorns (Kali Mirch) - Adds gentle heat and aids digestion
5. Carom Seeds (Ajwain) - Offers sharp, herbal punch and digestive benefits

### Ground Spices:
1. Dried Mango Powder (Amchur) - Brings the signature sour tang
2. Dried Ginger Powder (Saunth) - Adds warmth and subtle sharpness
3. Asafoetida (Hing) - Gives savory umami depth
4. Black Salt (Kala Namak) - Essential for sulfurous, bold chaat flavor (KEY INGREDIENT)
5. Pink Salt or Regular Salt - Balances sharp flavors
6. Kashmiri Red Chili Powder - Adds mild heat and color
7. Sugar (optional) - Mellows sharp tones

### The Defining Ingredient:
Black Salt (Kala Namak) is what defines authentic chat masala. Take it away and you no longer have chat masala!

# 3. HOW TO USE CHAT MASALA

## Golden Rule:
Add chat masala AT THE END, just before serving. Heat dulls the aromatic compounds and flattens the sourness.

## Best Uses:

### On Chaat and Street Snacks:
- Dahi baray, Fruit chaat, Papri chaat
- Aloo tikki, Samosa, Pakora
- Golgappa/Pani Puri, Bhel puri
- Katori chaat, Raj Kachori

### On Fresh Fruit and Salads:
- Melon, pineapple, mango, apple, guava
- Cucumber-and-onion salad, Fresh fruit platters

### On Grilled Meats, Eggs and Fries:
- Seekh kebab, tikka, grilled chicken
- Boiled or fried eggs, French fries/masala fries

### In Raita, Yogurt and Drinks:
- Raita, Lassi, Fresh lemonade, Chaas (buttermilk)

## Dosage:
Start with about half a teaspoon per serving and adjust. Chat masala is concentrated - a little goes a long way.

# 7. CHAT MASALA vs GARAM MASALA

Chat Masala = Finishing spice, Tangy/sour/salty, Add at end, Street foods/fruits, Key: Black salt/amchur
Garam Masala = Cooking spice, Warm/aromatic, Add during cooking, Curries/rice/meat, Key: Cardamom/cinnamon

They are NOT interchangeable!

# 8. STORAGE TIPS

- Store in airtight container
- Keep in cool, dark, dry place
- Away from sunlight and heat
- Do NOT refrigerate
- Use dry spoon only
- Lasts up to 1 year
